# The Spider in the Sink 🕷️💛

**A Mini Moments Matter Story**
*Series: Curious Moments (2–3) / Big Little Adventures (3–5) — adaptable
Draft by: Owner — original unpublished draft, 2026*

---

It was a very ordinary morning.

The children were playing.

Some were building.

Some were talking.

Some were busy making their own little plans for the day.

And then someone noticed something in the sink.

"Teacher!"

A little finger pointed.

"What's that?"

Everyone stopped.

There, tucked inside the sink, was a tiny spider.

It wasn't on the activity plan.

It wasn't part of the morning schedule.

It wasn't something anyone had expected to find.

But suddenly... everyone was interested.

Little faces gathered around.

Tiny hands pointed.

Curious eyes watched.

And the questions began.

"What's that?"

"Where did it come from?"

"Does it have a mommy?"

"Where is it going?"

The teacher looked at the spider.

Then she looked at the children.

There was something wonderful happening.

They weren't just looking at a spider.

They were wondering.

So instead of rushing past the moment, they paused.

"Let's take a closer look."

The children watched carefully.

Some children wondered how many legs the spider had.

Some wondered where it might be going.

Someone thought it might be looking for its family.

Someone else wondered if it was lost.

And suddenly, one tiny spider had opened the door to a very big world.

"What do spiders eat?"

"Where do they live?"

"How do they make webs?"

"Can spiders climb?"

"Are all spiders the same?"

There were questions... and more questions... and even more questions.

The spider had become their teacher for the morning.

Later, the children pretended to be spiders.

They crawled.

They stretched.

They moved carefully around their imaginary webs.

They sang songs about spiders.

They created webs with lines and patterns.

They searched outside for tiny creatures.

They looked under leaves.

They counted legs.

They compared colours and sizes.

They made up stories about where their little creatures might be going.

Nobody had planned all of this.

It started with one tiny visitor.

A spider in the sink.

And that was the wonderful thing about being curious.

One question can lead to another.

One discovery can lead to an adventure.

One tiny moment can open an entire world.

## 🕷️ Mini Moment Reflection

After the adventure, sit together and wonder:

What do you think the spider was doing in the sink?

Where do you think it was trying to go?

What would you ask the spider if it could answer you?

What was your favourite thing we discovered?

And perhaps the most important question:

What else could we wonder about?

## 🔍 Let's Go Looking

The next time you're outside, slow down.

Don't just look for the big things.

Look for the tiny ones.

A little bug.

A spider web.

A worm.

A leaf.

A seed.

A tiny flower.

A feather.

Something hiding beneath a rock.

Choose one thing to investigate together.

Look closely.

Ask questions.

Make guesses.

Listen to the ideas children come up with.

There doesn't need to be a right answer immediately.

Sometimes the wondering is the learning.

## 🎨 Let's Create Our Own Web

Can you make a spider web?

Try drawing one.

Build one with yarn.

Create lines with paint.

Make one with sticks outside.

Or use your bodies to become a giant spider web together.

Then ask:

"What happens when we change the shape?"

"How could we make it bigger?"

"Can you find a way through?"

## 🌱 As You Grow...

Today, the question might be: "Where did the spider come from?"

Later, it might become: "Why does a spider need a web?"

Then: "How do different spiders build different webs?"

And someday: "How do spiders fit into an ecosystem?"

The spider hasn't changed the world.

But the child's ability to understand the world has changed.

And that is the beauty of revisiting a Mini Moment.

The same curiosity can grow into new questions.

The three-year-old who once wondered where the spider's mommy was might one day become the child who wants to know how the whole ecosystem works.

The tiny visitor was only there for a moment.

But the wonder it created can keep growing.
