# The Floating Parachute 🪂💛

**A Mini Moments Matter Story**
*Series: Big Little Adventures (3–5) — adaptable
Draft by: Content team — original unpublished draft, 2026*

---

Theo rolled a paper plate into a little bowl.

His grown-up cut four strings.

And they tied the strings to the corners, hanging down like four spider legs.

"What now?" Theo asked.

"Now," said his grown-up, "we give it something to carry."

Theo thought.

He ran to his room and came back with a tiny bear.

They tied the bear to the strings, right in the middle.

"Ready?" said the grown-up.

Theo held it up high.

He let go.

And the little parachute—

hung there, for a breath, like it was deciding—

then drifted down, slow and swaying, like a leaf that had all the time in the world.

The bear landed soft as a snowflake.

"Again!" Theo shouted. "Again!"

And so they flew it again.

And again.

Theo climbed on a chair. Higher.

He dropped it. It floated down, slower this time, spinning a little.

"Why does it float?" Theo asked.

"Let's find out," said his grown-up. "What happens if we try without the plate?"

They tied the strings to the bear with no plate at all.

Theo dropped it.

Down it went — fast, like a stone.

"It fell straight down," Theo said.

"So what does the plate do?" his grown-up asked.

"It's the floater," Theo said. "It catches the air and holds us up."

"Like an umbrella," said his grown-up. "Or a real parachute."

Theo's eyes went wide.

"You mean people use these?" he said.

"Real ones," said his grown-up. "To float safely down from high, high up."

Theo looked at his paper plate like it had just told him a secret.

"Let's drop it from the tallest place," he whispered.

They went to the top of the stairs.

The grown-up held the landing. Theo leaned over the edge and let go.

The little parachute opened, caught the hall air, and floated, floated, floated down—

right past Nana, who looked up and laughed.

"Well," she said, catching it in her hands, "I've never been visited by a flying bear before."

Theo giggled.

"It was a parachute!" he said. "An air-catcher!"

"A very good one," Nana said. "Shall we fly it from the window together?"

They did.

They flew the bear parachute out over the garden, again and again, watching it catch the air and come home.

You don't need to fly to know what flying feels like.

You need a paper plate, some string, and someone who will wonder about the air with you.

## 💛 Mini Moment Reflection

After your flying afternoon, sit together and wonder:

What did the parachute do when you first let it go?

Which drop floated the slowest — and why do you think that was?

What changed when we took the plate away?

If you could put anything tiny in the parachute, what would you send flying?

And then:

What else in our world floats on the air — a feather, a leaf, a bird?

## 🎨 Make Your Own Flying Parachute

Ready to make something that floats? You'll need:

🟤 A paper plate
✂️ Scissors — grown-up can help
🧵 Four pieces of string or yarn
🧸 Something small and light to carry — a toy or a cork

Here's how:

1. Roll the plate into a shallow bowl, or use it flat and poke four holes near the edge.
2. Tie a piece of string to each hole.
3. Tie all four strings together at the bottom.
4. Attach your small passenger to the knot.

Now test it — an explorer's question:

"Which drop makes it float the longest: high, low, or from a spin?"

Try each one and watch closely.

You're not just playing. You're finding out how the air works.

## 🌱 As You Grow...

When you're little, a parachute is a paper plate and a bear, and floating is magical.

As you grow, you might begin to wonder: What does the air actually do?

Then: Why does a big, wide thing fall more slowly than a small one?

Then: How do real parachutes — and birds, and seeds — use the air to stay up?

Maybe one day you'll learn about drag, and air, and the clever shapes that slow things down.

Maybe you'll discover that the wandering leaf and the floating parachute follow the very same secret.

The plate stays a plate. But the questions grow with you.

And that's what makes this moment worth remembering.

Because someday, you might look at a paper plate and remember the afternoon you and a little bear learned to fly.

And maybe someone will tell you: "You were curious about how the world floats when you were little."

Maybe that parachute was the beginning of a love of how things lift and fall.

Maybe it wasn't.

We don't have to know. We just have to let go and watch.

## 🌟 The Tiny Moment

It wasn't part of anyone's plan.

No one had scheduled a flight.

It was simply... a paper plate, four strings, a bear, and a child who let go.

Someone helped tie it.

Someone leaned over the stairs with him.

Someone caught it mid-air and laughed.

And for a little while, an ordinary house had a sky in it.

That's the magic of childhood.

You don't need a real plane to fly.

You need a little shape, a little string, and someone who'll wonder about the air right beside you.
