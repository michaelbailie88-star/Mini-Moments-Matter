# The Tray of Seeds 🌻💛

**A Mini Moments Matter Story**
*Series: Growing Minds (5–7+) — adaptable
Draft by: Content team — original unpublished draft, 2026*

---

"Who do you think will come?" Lily asked.

She smoothed the peanut butter over the paper plate with a spoon — slow, careful, like she was icing a tiny cake.

"The birds will decide," said Nana. "We just get to wait and see."

Lily scattered the seeds on, pressing them gently so they stayed.

Then Nana tied their little tray to the garden fence, just at the height of Lily's eyes.

"How long will they take?" Lily asked.

"They might come today," said Nana. "Or tomorrow. Or the day after."

Lily stared at the tray.

Nothing came.

She checked after breakfast. Nothing.

She checked after lunch. Still nothing.

"The birds don't know it's for them yet," said Nana. "They have to find it. Finding is part of the feast."

Lily thought about that.

Then she did something small.

She stopped checking.

She went off and raced her scooter around the garden, and climbed the low branch, and forgot the tray was even there.

And then—

a flash of grey.

A little bird landed on the fence, cocked its head at the tray, and hopped down.

It took one seed. Then another. Then it grabbed two at once, like it was in a hurry for a very important appointment.

"Look!" Lily whispered from the step. "LOOK!"

"Look," Nana whispered back. "The waiting worked."

They watched from very still, like two statues that happened to be very happy.

The bird ate. Then it flew off with a seed held tight.

"Do you think it's telling its friends?" Lily asked.

"I think," said Nana, "it's taking a little seed to someone it shares with."

Lily's eyes went wide.

"The bird has a friend?"

"Maybe a family," said Nana. "Wild things are gentle like that."

They waited again.

Three more birds came — two little grey ones and a red-chested one who seemed very sure of himself.

Lily watched them take turns.

"I like sharing with them," she said softly. "We didn't even need to talk."

"You gave them food," said Nana. "They gave you their company. That's a fair trade."

By evening, the tray was nearly empty, and the garden had a new quiet in it — the quiet of things that have been fed and are satisfied.

"Will you fill it again tomorrow?" Lily asked.

"Shall we?" said Nana.

"Yes," said Lily. "Because they know where it is now. They'll be back."

"Kindness is like that," said Nana. "You put a little out into the world, and the world learns where to find it."

Lily looked at the empty tray in the soft evening light.

"It's not empty," she said. "It's waiting."

You don't need to raise a whole flock to feed a wild thing.

You need a tray, some seeds, and the patience to let the world find the good you put out.

## 💛 Mini Moment Reflection

After your feeding day, sit together and wonder:

How did it feel to wait, and wait, and then finally see a bird come?

What did you notice about how the very first bird moved and checked?

Why do you think the birds came back — how did they know?

Why do you think sharing with wild things matters even when no one is watching?

And then:

What else in our world might come to us if we put a little kindness out and waited?

## 🎨 Let's Make a Tray of Seeds

Ready to feed your garden? You'll need:

🟤 A paper plate
🥄 Peanut butter, or another spread (check allergies first!) — a grown-up helps
🌻 A handful of birdseed
🧶 String or yarn, to tie it up

Here's how:

1. Spread the peanut butter over the plate, all the way to the edges.
2. Press the seeds in gently, covering the whole tray.
3. Tie a string through the plate and hang it where you can see it from a window.
4. Wait — really wait. Let the birds find it themselves.
5. Keep a little visitor list: who came today, what they looked like, what they took.

A grown-up's "why" question:

"What do you think the birds are doing when they cock their heads and look at our tray?"

Let your child guess — then watch to see if they were right.

## 🌱 As You Grow...

When you're little, a bird feeder is a tray of seeds, and waiting is the hardest and best part.

As you grow, you might begin to wonder: How do birds find food in new places?

Then: What do different birds eat, and why do some stay all winter?

Then: How do wild creatures and people share a world, kindly?

Maybe one day you'll learn about migration, and seasons, and every clever way animals find what they need.

Maybe you'll discover that a tray of seeds was your first lesson in patience — and the first kindness you aimed at the wild.

The tray stays a tray. But the questions grow with you.

And that's what makes this moment worth remembering.

Because someday, you might watch a bird at a feeder and remember the afternoon you learned to wait for a flash of grey.

And maybe someone will tell you: "You fed the world gently when you were growing."

Maybe that tray was the beginning of a lifetime of caring for wild things.

Maybe it wasn't.

We don't have to know. We just have to put a little out and wait.

## 🌟 The Tiny Moment

It wasn't part of anyone's plan.

No one had scheduled a bird.

It was simply... a paper plate, some seeds, and a child who stopped checking long enough to let the world arrive.

Someone showed her how.

Someone kept her company while nothing came.

Someone whispered "look" at exactly the right moment.

And for a little while, a garden fence held a feast that brought the sky down to say hello.

That's the magic of growing up.

Kindness is like a tray of seeds.

You put a little out into the world, wait patiently... and the world learns where to find you.
