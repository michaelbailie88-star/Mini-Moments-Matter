# The Flower Paint 🌸💛

**A Mini Moments Matter Story**
*Series: Big Little Adventures (3–5) — adaptable
Draft by: Content team — original unpublished draft, 2026*

---

"I'm painting my favourite flower," said Lily.

She held a coffee filter flat on the tray — white, round, and plain.

She coloured it with a marker: a pink circle in the middle, and pink lines reaching out to the edges like petals.

"Now the magic," said Nana, and she handed Lily a little cup of water and a paintbrush.

Lily touched the brush to the pink.

Nothing happened.

Then the water soaked in, slow and spreading, like colour taking a deep breath.

And the pink began to bloom.

It bled outward, past the lines she'd drawn, over and through, until the whole filter was soft with rose.

"It's alive," Lily breathed.

"Watch the petals," said Nana.

The pink crept to the edges, wet and glowing, and the plain filter became a flower.

Lily made another.

This time yellow in the middle, with red lines, and a dot of blue where no one told her what to put.

The water carried the colours together, and where the yellow met the red, something new appeared.

"Orange!" Lily gasped. "I didn't even draw orange!"

"No," said Nana softly. "The colours made it themselves."

"Do colours do that?" Lily asked.

"Some colours, when they meet," said Nana. "Let's see which other ones can."

So they tested.

Blue and yellow — and out came green.

Red and blue — and there was purple, shy at first, then bolder.

Lily painted a whole garden.

A pink one, a yellow-and-red one, a blue mystery one.

She hung each wet flower on the line to dry, and the kitchen turned into a blooming field.

"There's my garden," Lily said, standing back. "It grew right out of my fingers."

"What's your favourite flower in it?" Nana asked.

Lily walked along the line, looking at each one.

Then she pointed to the blue mystery.

"That one," she said. "Because nobody told it what to be. It just painted itself."

Nana looked at the mystery flower, all soft blues and unexpected edges.

"Some of the best flowers," she said, "are the ones that surprise you."

Lily grinned.

"Do you think the colours knew?" she asked. "Before they met?"

"I think," said Nana, "that's what we just found out together."

The garden dried on the line all afternoon, light in the window.

You don't need a yard to grow a garden.

You need a circle of paper, some water, and the patience to let colour find its own way.

## 🎨 Let's Grow a Paper Flower Garden

Ready to grow flowers that bloom with water? You'll need:

🟤 Round coffee filters — one flower each
🖍️ Washable markers in your favourite colours
💧 A cup of water and a paintbrush
☀️ A line or tray for drying

Here's how:

1. Draw in the middle of the filter — a circle of colour, lines reaching out like petals.
2. Dip your brush in water. Touch it gently to the middle.
3. Watch the colour creep outward, blooming as it goes. Don't rush it.
4. Add more dots of colour while it's wet, and see where they bleed to.
5. Hang your flowers to dry, and make a whole garden.

A grown-up's "why" question:

"Where do you think the new colour — orange, or green, or purple — comes from?"

Let them wonder. The answer is theirs to discover.

## 🌱 As You Grow...

When you're little, a flower is a coloured circle that blooms when water touches it — and that's the whole wonder.

As you grow, you might begin to wonder: Why does the colour travel through wet paper?

Then: Why do blue and yellow make green?

Then: What else in our world is made when separate things come together?

Maybe one day you'll learn about colour, and how water spreads, and the surprising ways things mix.

Maybe you'll discover that a paper garden was the first place you watched something become more than the sum of its parts.

The filters stay filters. But the questions grow with you.

And that's what makes this moment worth remembering.

Because someday, you might hold a soft, faded paper flower and remember the afternoon a garden grew across your kitchen.

And maybe someone will tell you: "You loved watching colours surprise you when you were little."

Maybe that garden was the beginning of a love of making and mixing and creating.

Maybe it wasn't.

We don't have to know. We just have to watch it bloom.

## 🌟 The Tiny Moment

It wasn't part of anyone's plan.

No one had scheduled a garden.

It was simply... a circle of white paper, a scribbled pink, and a brush of water.

Someone handed over a cup of water.

Someone watched the colour run.

Someone hung every wet flower up to dry.

And for a while, an ordinary kitchen grew a field of paper blooms.

That's the magic of childhood.

You don't need a garden to grow flowers.

You need a round piece of paper, a little water, and the patience to let colour find its own way.
