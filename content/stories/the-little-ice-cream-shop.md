# The Little Ice Cream Shop 🍨💛

**A Mini Moments Matter Story**
*Series: Big Little Adventures (3–5) — adaptable
Draft by: Content team — original unpublished draft, 2026*

---

The kitchen table became a shop.

Lily rolled a ball of playdough between her palms and set it in a bowl like a scoop of ice cream.

One scoop, pink.

Another, brown.

Another, so pale it must have been vanilla.

"Welcome to my ice cream shop," she announced. "You may have one scoop."

"One scoop, please," said Nana, sitting down at the table.

"How much is it?" asked Theo, pulling up a chair.

"Five buttons," said Lily.

Theo dug in his pocket and handed over five pretend buttons, one by one.

Lily counted them carefully.

"One, two, three, four, five," she said. "Here is your ice cream."

She scooped a pink ball into the bowl and handed it over.

"Thank you," said Theo. He took a bite of air, and his face went bright.

"Oh!" he said. "It's strawberry! The strawberry-est!"

"Would you like a sprinkle?" asked Lily.

"Yes!"

She sprinkled pretend sprinkles from a pretend jar, shaking it exactly the way a real shop does.

Nana leaned in.

"Could I try a cone with two scoops?" she asked. "If it's not too much trouble."

"It's not too much trouble," said Lily importantly, "because I am the boss of the shop."

She rolled two scoops, stacked them on a pretend cone, and held it out.

Nana took the smallest nibble of air.

"Mmm," she said, closing her eyes. "That's the best ice cream in the whole world."

Theo giggled.

"You didn't even have it yet," he said. "That's pretend, silly."

"Pretend or not," said Nana, "it tastes like sunshine."

And then the shop got very busy.

Theo wanted a chocolate cone with sprinkles and a paper umbrella.

Lily made it, then made one for herself.

The bear came in and wanted a bowl with no scoop, just a spoon.

Lily made it, because every bear is welcome.

"How much?" asked Theo, coming back for a second.

"The same," said Lily. "Five buttons."

"I only have three," said Theo.

"Then you get a baby scoop," said Lily. "That's the rule."

Theo accepted the baby scoop with great seriousness.

And the little shop kept serving, scoop after scoop, until the playdough was warm and smooth from rolling and the kitchen table was entirely covered in pretend.

At the end, Theo counted his buttons.

"Come back tomorrow," said Lily.

"We will," said Nana. "Best customer service in the house."

You don't need real ice cream to run the best shop in the world.

You need a handful of playdough, a pocket of buttons, and people who take their pretend seriously.

## 💛 Mini Moment Reflection

After your ice cream afternoon, sit together and wonder:

What was the very first scoop in your shop?

Who was your favourite customer — and what did they order?

What happens when someone doesn't have enough buttons?

What rule would you make for your own shop?

And then:

What else could our table become tomorrow — a bakery, a post office, a zoo?

## 🎨 Let's Open Our Own Little Shop

Ready to open for business? You'll need:

🔴 Playdough in a few colours — your scoops
🍦 Bowls, cups, or a paper cone
🧺 Something to count for payment — buttons, stones, or pasta shells

Here's how:

1. Roll the playdough into balls — the scoops of your shop.
2. Set up your table like a counter. Decide what you sell.
3. Take turns being the shopkeeper and the customer.
4. Pay with pretend buttons, counting them out one by one.
5. Sprinkle pretend sprinkles, too. Sprinkles make everything better.

A grown-up's "why" question:

"Why do you think it's fun to pretend to buy and sell?"

Let your child answer — then answer yourself.

The rule of the shop: everyone who asks, gets served.

## 🌱 As You Grow...

When you're little, a shop is a table and some playdough, and the rule is whatever you say.

As you grow, you might begin to wonder: What do shops really do?

Then: How do you know what something is worth?

Then: What happens when you trade, and save, and count?

Maybe one day you'll learn about money, and maths, and the give-and-take that runs the world.

Maybe you'll discover that turn-taking and counting buttons were quiet practice for all of it.

The shop stays a shop. But the questions grow with you.

And that's what makes this moment worth remembering.

Because someday, you might find a button in a pocket and remember the afternoon you were the boss of a kitchen-table shop.

And maybe someone will tell you: "You counted every button carefully when you were little."

Maybe that shop was the beginning of a love of making and trading and fairness.

Maybe it wasn't.

We don't have to know. We just have to serve.

## 🌟 The Tiny Moment

It wasn't part of anyone's plan.

No one had scheduled a shop.

It was simply... a ball of playdough, a bowl, and a child who decided the kitchen table was open for business.

Someone sat down to order.

Someone counted out buttons.

Someone took a tiny bite of air and said it tasted like sunshine.

And for a while, an ordinary table became the busiest corner of the whole house.

That's the magic of childhood.

You don't need real money to run a real store.

You need a little roll, a little count, and someone who will take your pretend seriously.
