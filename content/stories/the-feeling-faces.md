# The Feeling Faces 🙂😡💛

**A Mini Moments Matter Story**
*Series: Curious Moments (2–3) — adaptable
Draft by: Content team — original unpublished draft, 2026*

---

It was just another afternoon.

The toys were out.

The songs were sung.

And then a little face crumpled.

Not a big fall. Not a big ow.

Just... a feeling.

A wobbly, stormy, no-words sort of feeling.

It arrived the way feelings do.

All at once, with no warning, wearing a very big frown.

You wanted to say something.

But the words weren't ready.

So Nana did something surprising.

She didn't say "don't cry."

She didn't say "what's wrong?"

She just sat down on the floor.

She put a little stack of cards between you—and on each card, a face.

A happy face, round and sunny.

A sad face, with droopy eyes.

A cross face, with a scrunched-up nose.

A surprised face, with a big round mouth.

And a sleepy face, soft and yawny.

"Show me," Nana said, "which face is in your tummy today."

And you looked.

It wasn't the happy face.

It wasn't the sleepy face.

You pointed...

at the cross face.

"Cross," you said.

And just like that, the big feeling had a name.

Nana didn't fix it.

She didn't say "let's be happy now."

She just nodded.

"Cross," she said back. "That's a big one."

Then she made the cross face herself.

Eyes scrunched. Nose pinched. Cheeks puffed up like a thundercloud.

"Look at me," she said. "I'm cross too."

And you giggled.

Just a little.

Because the face was silly, and also because it was true—someone knew.

Then Nana asked the question with no pressure.

"What does cross look like?"

You showed her—a big scrunch, just like the card.

"What does cross sound like?"

You made a little growly noise, and Nana made a bigger one, and it was funny.

"Where do you feel cross?"

You touched your tummy.

"Tummy," you said.

And the feeling was still there—but it wasn't hiding anymore.

It was on the floor between you, on a card, with a name and a face and a sound.

The moment passed, the way feelings do.

The cloud wandered off.

The happy face got its turn.

And Nana stayed sitting on the floor with you, the whole way through.

"Feelings come and go," she said. "They're all allowed in here."

She tapped the cards.

"They're all yours."

That's the thing about naming a feeling.

It doesn't make it go away.

But it does something almost as good.

It makes it smaller. It makes it known. It makes it yours.

And a feeling that's yours, you can feel—and let pass.

## 🙂 Mini Moment Reflection

After your feeling-faces game, sit together and wonder:

Which face was in your tummy today?

What does cross look like? What does sad look like?

What does the happy face do to your whole body?

Where do you feel your feelings — tummy, chest, hands?

And then:

What is one thing that makes your happy face come out?

## 🎭 Let's Play the Face Game

You'll need your faces (your own are best — you can use them anywhere).

Here's how to play:

🙂 Ask: "Can you show me happy?" and make the biggest happy face you can.
😢 Show sad with droopy shoulders and a soft, droopy voice.
😡 Make cross with a scrunch and a growl — then laugh at how silly it got.
😮 Make surprised with a giant round "whoa!"
😴 Finish with sleepy, slow and yawny.

Then flip it around:

The grown-up makes a face, and you guess what it is.

Then name the feeling that's actually in the room right now.

Some days it's happy. Some days it's cross, or tired, or a bit of everything.

That's okay.

All the faces are allowed. All the faces are yours.

## 🎨 Draw a Feeling Face

Can you draw a feeling?

Pick one — happy, sad, cross, surprised, or sleepy.

🖍️ Draw the eyes — round? scrunched? droopy?
🖍️ Draw the mouth — a smile? a frown? a big round O?
🖍️ Give it eyebrows if it wants them.

Then give your feeling a name and a colour.

What colour is cross?

What colour is sleepy?

Your drawing can live on the fridge — a reminder that all feelings have faces, and all faces are welcome here.

## 🌱 As You Grow...

When you're little, a feeling is a scrunched-up face with no words.

As you grow, you might begin to wonder: Why do I feel cross?

Then: What makes the feeling come and go?

Then: What can help when the feeling is very big?

Maybe one day you'll learn about why bodies feel what they feel.

Maybe you'll discover that naming a feeling really does help — that words give a storm somewhere to land.

Maybe you'll notice that everyone you love has feeling faces too.

The feeling stays a feeling. But the words grow with you.

And that's what makes this moment worth remembering.

Because someday, you might look back and remember the afternoon you pointed at a card and told someone what was in your tummy.

And maybe someone will tell you: "You learned to name your feelings when you were little."

Maybe that card was the beginning of a lifetime of knowing yourself.

Maybe it wasn't.

We don't have to know. We just have to name it.

## 🙂 The Tiny Moment

It wasn't part of anyone's plan.

There was no lesson about emotions on the schedule.

It was simply... a crumpled face and a stack of little cards.

But someone stopped.

Someone sat down on the floor.

Someone said, "Show me which face is in your tummy today."

And for a little while, a big wobbly feeling became a card you could hold, a name you could say, a face you could make.

That's the magic of childhood.

Feelings are big when they're nameless.

But put a name on one, and it fits in your hand.

Every feeling is allowed. Every feeling has a face. And every face belongs to you.
