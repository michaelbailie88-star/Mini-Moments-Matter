# The Reading Race 📚💛

**A Mini Moments Matter Story**
*Series: Growing Minds (5–7+) — adaptable
Draft by: Content team — original unpublished draft, 2026*

---

"Ready?" asked the grown-up.

Theo picked up the book.

"Ready," he said.

"Then go."

Theo read.

The words came out in a careful line — steady, a little slow, pulling each sound into place the way he'd been practising.

"Time!" said the grown-up.

"How many?" Theo asked.

"Eighteen words," said the grown-up. "In one minute. That's a really strong start."

"Only eighteen?" said Theo.

"Remember yesterday?" said the grown-up. "You read fourteen."

Theo was quiet.

Then his face lit up.

"Fourteen! And now I can read EIGHTEEN?"

"Four more words," said the grown-up. "In just one day."

Theo looked at the book like it had grown a new page.

"So I'm faster than yesterday," he said.

"You are," said the grown-up. "You're racing one person — and it's not me, and it's not your friend. It's yesterday-you."

"Yesterday-me," Theo repeated, liking it.

They did it again.

Theo read the same little page, pushing gently, letting the words find their rhythm.

"Twenty-one!" the grown-up counted.

Theo jumped off the sofa.

"TWENTY-ONE! I beat yesterday-me TWICE!"

"Wait," said the grown-up, laughing, "one minute ago it was yesterday-me. Now it's... you, from a minute ago."

Theo thought.

"So I beat myself?"

"Every single time you read," said the grown-up, "you're racing the only runner that matters."

"Me, from before," said Theo.

"Me, from before," agreed the grown-up.

They kept going until the words came quicker and smoother — until eighteen became twenty-three, and twenty-three nearly became twenty-six.

"The words got easy," Theo said, half surprised.

"They were always waiting for you," said the grown-up. "They just like being read."

Theo hugged the book.

"Maybe tomorrow," he said, "I'll read twenty-six. Or thirty."

"Maybe you will," said the grown-up. "And maybe you won't. Some days we read faster, and some days we just read. The race is still worth running."

Theo nodded.

"Tomorrow," he said, "I'm racing me-from-today."

"Good," said the grown-up. "You're going to be the best competition you ever had."

You don't need a prize to win a reading race.

You need a minute, a book, and the quiet joy of being faster than the you from yesterday.

## 💛 Mini Moment Reflection

After your reading race, sit together and wonder:

How many words did you read in your first minute today?

How many for the one before — and what does the gap tell you?

What did it feel like to race yesterday-you instead of someone else?

Which words are your favourites to say out loud?

And then:

What else could "yesterday-me" help you practice — jumping, building, drawing?

## 🎨 The One-Minute Reading Race

Ready to race yourself? You'll need:

📖 A book or a page you can read
⏱️ A timer or phone — one minute
📝 A pencil and paper, to keep your scores

Here's how:

1. Choose a page you like. Pick the same page every time — that's how you see yourself grow.
2. Set the timer for one minute. Read out loud, at your own pace.
3. When the minute ends, count the words you read. Write the number down.
4. Try once more. Did the number grow?
5. Keep a little score chart. Each day you race, see how you did against yesterday-you.

A grown-up's "why" question:

"Why do you think practising the same thing a little every day makes it get easier?"

Let your child find their own answer — then read together, just because.

## 🌱 As You Grow...

When you're little, a reading race is words in a minute, and beating yesterday is the whole thrill.

As you grow, you might begin to wonder: Why does the same page get easier over time?

Then: How does practice actually change the way we learn?

Then: What else can grow just a little, every single day?

Maybe one day you'll learn about memory, and practice, and how small daily steps build into big skills.

Maybe you'll discover that the reading race taught you something bigger than reading — the joy of competing with no one but yourself.

The books stay books. But the questions grow with you.

And that's what makes this moment worth remembering.

Because someday, you might read a whole novel in an afternoon and remember the minute when eighteen words was a victory.

And maybe someone will tell you: "You raced yourself, and you kept winning, when you were growing."

Maybe that race was the beginning of a lifetime of reading.

Maybe it wasn't.

We don't have to know. We just have to turn the page.

## 🌟 The Tiny Moment

It wasn't part of anyone's plan.

No one had scheduled a race.

It was simply... a minute, a book, and a child who read fourteen words yesterday.

Someone timed it.

Someone counted out loud.

Someone celebrated four extra words like they were a medal.

And for one golden minute, a slow reader discovered he was faster than the reader he was yesterday.

That's the magic of growing up.

You don't need to be the fastest in the room.

You need one minute, one page, and the quiet joy of out-reading the you from before.
