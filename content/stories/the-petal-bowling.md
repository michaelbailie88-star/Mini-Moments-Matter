# The Petal Bowling 🎳💛

**A Mini Moments Matter Story**
*Series: Big Little Adventures (3–5) — adaptable
Draft by: Content team — original unpublished draft, 2026*

---

The whole hall was empty.

"This is our lane," said Nana. "Want to play?"

Theo looked at the lane.

There were no balls. No pins.

Just a row of paper plates standing on their edges, three and three and three, like petals on a flower.

"Those are your pins," said Nana. "Set them like petals — that's why we call it petal bowling."

"Are they plates?" asked Theo.

"They're pins," said Nana. "Watch."

She stood behind the line.

She held the soft ball.

And she rolled it straight down the lane.

The ball wobbled, rolled, slowed...

and knocked over four plates with a happy clatter.

"Four!" Nana counted. "Your turn."

Theo ran to the line.

He tossed the ball — high, not straight — and it bounced and landed in the middle of the pins, scattering two plates.

"Two!" counted Nana. "That's a two!"

"Only two?" said Theo.

"Only two," said Nana. "But look at how they fell — one knocked the next, and that one knocked another two. So you knocked four."

"Did I?" Theo looked at the scattered plates. "So two is really four?"

"Counting," said Nana, "is full of surprises."

They set the pins again, petals all in a row.

Theo tried rolling low, like Nana had.

The ball went straight and true.

CLATTER! Five pins down.

"FIVE!" Theo shouted. "I got five!"

"You got five," said Nana, and she clapped.

Theo aimed at the last standing pin, alone on the lane.

The ball crept over, nudged the plate, and the plate tipped over with one last quiet clatter.

"Everything fell!" Theo cheered.

He set the pins back up all by himself.

"Again?" he asked.

"Again," said Nana.

They bowled until the plates were worn soft at the edges.

They counted pins until both their voices had the same happy rhythm in them.

One. Two. Three. Four. Five. Six.

When the final roll was done — six out of six — Theo stood behind the line and looked at the empty lane.

"I want to be the pin-setter now," he said.

So Nana bowled, and Theo set the pins, catching each fallen plate and standing it back up, petals in a row again.

"You're the best pin-setter in the house," said Nana, rolling her third strike in a row — or so she said.

"Are strikes real?" asked Theo.

"Strikes," said Nana, "are when a pretend game gets very, very real."

You don't need a bowling alley to bowl.

You need a row of paper petals, a soft ball, and someone who'll count every pin like it's a treasure.

## 💛 Mini Moment Reflection

After your bowling afternoon, sit together and wonder:

How many pins did you knock down on your best roll?

Did you notice that a toss and a roll land differently?

Which was stronger — rolling close to the ground, or throwing high?

What's your favourite number of pins to knock down?

And then:

What else could we roll at — and what could the pins be next time?

## 🎨 Let's Play Petal Bowling

Ready to bowl at home? You'll need:

🟤 Six or nine paper plates
✂️ Fold each one in half so it stands up like a tiny tent — a flower on its stem
🎾 One soft ball, or a rolled-up sock

Here's how:

1. Stand your plates in a row — or set them in a triangle, three, then two, then one.
2. Stand behind your line (a cushion or a strip of tape).
3. Roll the ball, don't toss it. Watch it slide close to the floor.
4. Count the pins that fall — count every single one.
5. Set them up again. Let everyone have a turn at rolling, and a turn at pin-setting.

A grown-up's "why" test:

"Try rolling the ball hard, then slow. Which knocks more pins down?"

That's a real experiment — and the pins are your results.

## 🌱 As You Grow...

When you're little, bowling is knocking things over, and counting the fall is the whole game.

As you grow, you might begin to wonder: Why do some balls roll straight and others wobble?

Then: Why does a low roll knock down more than a high throw?

Then: How do real bowlers aim, and score, and count?

Maybe one day you'll learn about forces, and angles, and the satisfying physics of things toppling.

Maybe you'll discover that petal bowling was early practice for aiming and counting and trying again.

The plates stay plates. But the questions grow with you.

And that's what makes this moment worth remembering.

Because someday, you might find a folded paper plate and remember the afternoon you knocked down six petals with one good roll.

And maybe someone will tell you: "You counted every single pin when you were little."

Maybe that lane was the beginning of a love of games and aims and friendly scores.

Maybe it wasn't.

We don't have to know. We just have to roll.

## 🌟 The Tiny Moment

It wasn't part of anyone's plan.

No one had scheduled a lane.

It was simply... a row of paper petals, a soft ball, and a child who aimed.

Someone stood behind the line with him.

Someone counted out loud, two, three, five.

Someone let him set the pins back up again.

And for a while, an empty hallway was the flashiest alley in the whole world.

That's the magic of childhood.

You don't need a bowling alley to feel the thrill of a strike.

You need a row of folded plates, one good roll, and someone who'll count every pin you knock down.
