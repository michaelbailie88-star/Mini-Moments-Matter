# The Grumpy Morning 🌦️💛

**A Mini Moments Matter Story**
*Series: Curious Moments (2–3) — feelings & feelings
Draft by: Content team — original unpublished draft, 2026*

---

It was just another morning.

The sun came up.

The birds sang.

The toast was ready.

But someone had woken up grumpy.

You know the feeling.

Everything felt too loud.
Everything felt too bright.
Nothing felt right.

The socks were wrong.
The cereal was wrong.
The whole morning was wrong.

A little storm cloud rolled in and parked itself right over one small, grumpy person.

Grown-up looked down.

"Someone woke up feeling wobbly today," they said, very gently.

And they sat down close.

They didn't rush.
They didn't fix it.
They just stayed.

"It looks like there's a big wobbly feeling in there today," they said. "A big, twisty, grumpy feeling."

The little grumpy person nodded.

"This morning is too loud," they said.

"It's too bright," they said.

"The toast is too toasty," they said.

And the grown-up nodded slowly.

"Too loud," they said. "Too bright. Too toasty. My, those are big feelings for such a small morning."

And somehow, saying them out loud made them a little bit smaller.

"What does the feeling feel like?" the grown-up asked.

"Like a grumpy bear," said the little person.

"Like a storm," they said.

"Like a kicky," they said.

"A grumpy, stormy, kicky feeling," said the grown-up. "That's a lot to carry before breakfast."

So they carried it together.

That's the thing — grown-ups can help carry wobbly feelings, just for a little while, so they don't feel so heavy.

"Let me hold one corner of that feeling for you," said the grown-up.

And they made a big show of taking an invisible corner of the feeling and holding it in their own hands.

The little grumpy person giggled. Just a little.

Then the grown-up did what grown-ups can sometimes do.

"Would you like to see my grumpiest face?" they asked.

The little grumpy person nodded.

So the grown-up scrunched up their whole face.

Nose up. Eyes squished. Mouth all pulled sideways.

"So grumpy!" they growled.

The little grumpy person tried very hard not to laugh.

Then the grown-up made a second face — this time with the corners of the mouth pointing UP instead of down.

A silly, upside-down, not-grumpy-at-all face.

"Hmm," said the grown-up, "I think my grumpy face and my happy face got mixed up in the night."

And the little grumpy person laughed. Really laughed.

"A laugh!" said the grown-up. "I think the knot in the morning just got a little looser."

The storm cloud thinned.

The morning felt a little less loud.
A little less bright.
A little less toasty-wrong.

The grumpy feeling wasn't gone.

But it was smaller now. Lighter. Shared.

"Feelings don't stay forever," said the grown-up, gently. "They come, and they sit down, and sometimes they need us to name them. And then, little by little, they wander off."

And they did.

The grumpy morning slowly became... just a morning.

With socks that fit.
With cereal that tasted nice.
With a little person who was still a little wobbly sometimes — and who knew that was okay.

Somewhere under the grumpy feeling, a little spot of silliness had been hiding.

And a silly face had found it.

## 🌦️ Mini Moment Reflection

Sit together, once the wobbly feeling has quieted.

What was your wobbly feeling called today?

Grumpy?
Stormy?
Kicky?
Wobble-bottom?

What did it feel like in your body?

Where did you feel it — in your tummy? Your shoulders? Your hands?

What helped it get a little smaller?

And maybe the gentle question:

What does a happy feeling feel like in your body, so we can know the difference?

There's no wrong answer.

Feelings are just visitors. And it helps to give them a name.

## 🎭 The Name-the-Feeling Faces Game

Making a wobbly feeling smaller starts with giving it a name.

Here's a game to practice.

Draw or make faces together for each feeling:

😊 Happy — corners of the mouth up
😠 Grumpy — eyebrows pulled down
😢 Sad — a wobbling lip
😲 Surprised — big round eyes
😖 Frustrated — the squeeze-face

Take turns.

You make a face. Your little one names it.

Then they make a face. You name it.

Then try a silly one:

😜 The tongue-out face
🙃 The upside-down face
😝 The double-eyes-crossed face

Feel how the silly faces loosen the wobbly ones.

And when a big feeling comes again, you can say:

"Let's find its name."
"Show me the face that matches."

Because a feeling with a name is a feeling that's already a little bit smaller.

## 🌬️ The Slow Silly Breath

When the wobbly feeling is big, breathe together. Slowly. Silly.

Put a hand on your tummy.

In through the nose, out through the mouth.

In... like you're sniffing a flower.

Out... like you're cooling down a very hot piece of toast.

Now make it silly:

In through the nose... aaand out in one big, slow "puh!"
In... aaand out with your cheeks puffed like a frog.
In... aaand out with a long, slow, pretend-candle "shhh."

Three slow breaths.

That's all.

Feel how the shoulders soften.

Feel how the morning gets a little lighter.

You don't have to make the feeling go away.

You just have to be with it, and breathe, and know it will pass.

## 🌱 As You Grow...

When you're little, a bad morning feels enormous.

A storm cloud can cover the whole sky.

But there's a grown-up close by.

A grown-up who doesn't rush it.
A grown-up who helps you find the name for the feeling.
A grown-up who shows you a silly face and reminds you that feelings pass.

As you grow, you'll find your own ways.

You might name the feeling for yourself.
You might take a slow breath without being asked.
You might remember the silly face and make it on purpose.

The wobbly feelings will still come. They always do.

But now you'll know:

They have names.
They have shapes.
They pass.

And you are stronger than any storm cloud that parks over a morning.

That wobbly little moment? It taught you something.

It taught you that you can carry big feelings.

And you don't have to carry them alone.

## 🌦️ The Tiny Moment

It wasn't a happy morning.

Not at first.

It was loud and bright and toasty-wrong and full of a big wobbly feeling.

But someone paused.

Someone sat close.
Someone didn't try to fix it.
Someone helped name the feeling.

And then someone made a silly face.

A tiny little laugh.
A knot, loosened.
A storm cloud, thinner.

The grumpy morning didn't disappear.

But it got smaller. Shared. Nameable.

And somewhere under all that grump, a little spot of silliness was found.

That's the magic of a wobbly morning that someone stays for.

A feeling, named. A breath, taken. A laugh, found.
