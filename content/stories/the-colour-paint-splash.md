# The Colour Paint-Splash 🌈💛

**A Mini Moments Matter Story**
*Series: Curious Moments (2–3) — adaptable
Draft by: Content team — original unpublished draft, 2026*

---

It was just another rainy morning.

The clouds were low.

The rain tapped on the window.

And the whole morning was going to be indoors.

But on the table, there was a surprise waiting.

A white paper circle, soft and crinkly.

A little cup of water.

And a dot of red.

And a dot of blue.

And a dot of yellow.

You were about to dip your brush in.

But Papa stopped you.

"First," he said, "let's just watch one little thing."

He took the brush.

He dipped it in the water.

He touched one tiny corner of the white paper circle.

And then, in the middle—

he pressed one small dot of red.

Then he leaned back.

And you watched.

The red didn't stay a dot.

Slowly—so slowly—it opened.

It spread.

It bled into the white paper like a secret blooming.

"Whoa," you whispered.

"Watch," Papa said. "Now watch what happens if we let the red and blue meet."

He dipped the brush again. One dot of blue, right beside the red.

And then—

the edge of the red touched the edge of the blue.

And where they met, a whole new colour appeared.

You blinked.

"There!" Papa said, pointing. "Purple."

Not from a pot of paint.

Not from a box.

Purple, made right there on the paper, by the red and the blue touching.

You had to do it yourself.

You took the brush.

You chose the yellow.

You dipped. You dotted.

And you watched your own colour bloom.

The rain kept tapping.

The morning stayed indoors.

But on your paper, a whole garden was growing.

Red into pink.

Blue into green, where it reached the yellow.

Yellow into orange, where it touched the red.

You dotted and dotted.

You watched each new colour arrive like a visitor.

"Where did that come from?" you asked.

"The colours made it," Papa said. "They didn't know they could, until they met."

You thought about that.

Two colours, each fine on its own.

Together—something brand new.

You made more.

Dot after dot, edge touching edge.

The paper turned from white to a whole rainbow, all by itself.

The colour stopped moving when the paper was full.

The morning carried on.

The rain let up outside.

And your rainbow circle went up on the window, where the new sun shone through it.

It looked like a little stained-glass, all made by colour meeting colour.

"That's yours," Papa said. "You made it, and you didn't even tell the colours what to do. You just let them meet."

That's the thing about colour.

It spreads when you give it a little water and a little room.

It makes new things when it touches something different.

And the best part—

you never quite know what it'll become.

And then:

What else in our world changes when two things meet?

## 🖌️ Let's Make Colour Bloom

Grab a white coffee filter, some washable markers, a tiny cup of water, and a dropper.

Here's how to bloom your own colours:

🎨 Draw a few dots around the middle of your filter.
💧 Use the dropper to add a few drops of water to the middle.
👀 Watch the colours bleed outward, slow and soft.
🌈 Add one colour next to another and watch them meet.
🪄 Try drawing rings, or dots, or stripes — and see what blooms.

Leave it to dry.

Then hold it up to the light.

Every colour is doing something.

Every colour is meeting another colour.

No two filters will ever come out the same.

That's the whole point.

---

## 🌈 Make a Rainbow to Keep

Your bloomed filter can become something you keep.

Try this:

✂️ Fold your filter into petals to make a flower.
🖼️ Tape it to a window where the sun can shine through.
📦 Press it flat in a book, then hang it as a picture.

Give your creation a name:

"the purple that wasn't in the pot"
"the rainbow I made grow"
"the colour garden"

Then save it.

Your colour garden can live on for weeks.

## 🌱 As You Grow...

When you're little, the colours just spread, and you just watch.

As you grow, you might begin to wonder: Why does the red spread into the paper?

Then: Why does red and blue make purple?

Then: What makes one colour travel farther than another?

Maybe one day you'll learn about how colours mix, and how paper pulls water, and why some colours bloom faster than others.

Maybe you'll discover that mixing isn't just for paint — it's how lots of lovely things get made.

Maybe you'll notice that the red and blue were both needed to make the purple. Neither one did it alone.

The colours stay colours. But the questions grow with you.

And that's what makes this moment worth remembering.

Because someday, you might look at a photograph of yourself watching a colour bloom, leaning in, holding your breath, amazed that red and blue could become purple.

And maybe someone will tell you: "You were amazed by making things meet when you were little."

Maybe that first purple was the beginning of a lifelong wonder at how things combine.

Maybe it wasn't.

We don't have to know. We just have to watch.

## 🌈 The Tiny Moment

It wasn't part of anyone's plan.

It wasn't a science experiment or an art lesson.

It was simply... a drop of water, a dot of colour, and a paper circle.

But someone stopped.

Someone leaned in.

Someone let two colours meet, and watched something brand new arrive.

And for a little while, the rainy morning turned into a whole garden of surprises.

That's the magic of childhood.

Sometimes the most amazing things in the world are made by the simplest meetings.

Sometimes all it takes is two colours, a little water, and someone watching.
