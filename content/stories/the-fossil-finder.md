# The Fossil Finder 🦕💛

**A Mini Moments Matter Story**
*Series: Big Little Adventures (3–5) — adaptable
Draft by: Content team — original unpublished draft, 2026*

---

"Look," said Theo, holding up something gray and lumpy. "A fossil."

Nana looked at the gray, lumpy thing.

"Tell me about it," she said.

"It's a dinosaur tooth," said Theo. "From a T-Rex. He lost it millions of years ago when he was brushing."

"A T-Rex," said Nana, nodding. "With a toothbrush?"

"The little ones," said Theo seriously. "It's very old."

Nana set up the old playdough on the table and pressed a flat stone into it.

When she lifted it away, a perfect stone-shape was left behind in the dough.

"A fossil," she said. "See? The dinosaur leaves the shape of himself behind."

Theo went very quiet.

He picked up a leaf and pressed it into the playdough.

Lift.

A leaf-shape, with every tiny vein.

"It's him," he whispered. "The leaf is still here."

"That's what fossils are," said Nana. "The little print a creature leaves when it's gone."

Theo's hands began to dig.

He pressed Nana's button in — a circle. Her key — a key-shape. His own small finger — a tiny, curved print.

"Whose finger is that?" Nana asked.

"Mine," said Theo. "A hundred years from now, someone will find this and know a Theo lived here."

"A Theo-tops," said Nana.

Theo laughed so hard the playdough wobbled.

"A Theo-tops!" he repeated, and he pressed his finger in again, deeper this time, like a creature leaving his mark on the world.

They made a whole fossil field together.

Nana made a long, thin one with her palm edge. "An iguanodon foot," she explained.

Theo made three little digs in a row. "Raptor claws."

"Hold on," said Nana. "Something big is coming."

She pressed the whole playdough flat, laid the fossil stone in the centre, and pressed down hard with both thumbs.

A giant, bumpy print.

"A T-Rex," Theo breathed.

"He was here," said Nana. "Millions of years ago. And we just found him."

Theo looked at the print.

"What if it isn't a T-Rex?" he asked. "What if it's a mystery dinosaur nobody's ever found?"

"Then," said Nana, "we'd have to name him."

Theo thought very hard.

"Clawbert," he said at last.

"Clawbert the mystery dinosaur," said Nana. "This is the proudest discovery of the century."

They pressed a few more prints — a feather, a seashell, the corner of a crayon box.

Each print left its own little story in the dough.

And when the fossil field was full, Theo looked at Nana.

"Will people really find my finger print someday?" he asked.

Nana thought about it.

"The shape you leave behind," she said, "can last longer than the leaving. So be a kind print, Theo. That's my advice to a Theo-tops."

Theo pressed his finger in one last time.

"A kind print," he agreed. "And a T-Rex tooth."

You don't need to dig up bones to discover a dinosaur world.

You need a little playdough, a pressing hand, and someone who'll believe in Clawbert with you.

What was the very first thing you pressed into the dough?

What did the leaf leave behind that the leaf itself didn't have?

Whose finger do you think you'd find on a fossil from your family?

What would you name a mystery dinosaur no one has ever seen?

And then:

What story do you think the oldest fossil in the world is telling?

## 🎨 Let's Make Our Own Fossil Field

Ready to be a fossil finder? You'll need:

🟤 Playdough or flour-dough, rolled flat and smooth
🌿 Things to press — leaves, buttons, keys, shells, your own fingers
🦴 A flat stone or toy dinosaur, for the big prints

Here's how:

1. Roll your dough into a wide, flat field.
2. Choose something from your house — press it in. Don't wiggle!
3. Lift it away, very carefully. Look what it left behind.
4. Try your finger. Try your palm. Try the flat of a leaf.
5. Find a mystery: press a big shape, then name the creature that made it.

A grown-up's "why" question:

"Why do you think a print of a thing can stay even when the thing itself is gone?"

Let your child's answer be a clue — it's the first step of real palaeontology.

## 🌱 As You Grow...

When you're little, a fossil is a print in playdough, and finding one is magic.

As you grow, you might begin to wonder: How do real fossils form?

Then: What does a fossil tell us about a creature that lived long ago?

Then: How do scientists read the prints of the past?

Maybe one day you'll learn about rocks, and bones, and the long, slow story of life on Earth.

Maybe you'll discover that pressing a leaf into dough was the first time you ever "read" a print.

The dough stays dough. But the questions grow with you.

And that's what makes this moment worth remembering.

Because someday, you might hold a flat stone and remember the afternoon you left your own finger-print in a fossil field.

And maybe someone will tell you: "You read the past with your own two hands when you were little."

Maybe that dig was the beginning of a love of hidden histories.

Maybe it wasn't.

We don't have to know. We just have to press.

## 🌟 The Tiny Moment

It wasn't part of anyone's plan.

No one had scheduled a dig.

It was simply... a lump of playdough, a flat stone, and a child who lifted it up to find a shape.

Someone showed him how.

Someone believed in a tooth from a brushing T-Rex.

Someone named a mystery dinosaur Clawbert without giggling once.

And for a while, an ordinary table held a world that was millions of years old.

That's the magic of childhood.

You don't need a desert to find fossils.

You need something soft, something pressing, and someone who'll wonder what was here long, long ago.
