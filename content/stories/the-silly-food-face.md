# The Silly Food Face 🍝😄💛

**A Mini Moments Matter Story**
*Series: Curious Moments (2–3) — adaptable
Draft by: Content team — original unpublished draft, 2026*

---

It was just another afternoon at the table.

The dough was out — soft, warm, a little lumpy.

And the plan was to make food with it.

Little sausages. Little peas. Little carrots.

That's what the grown-up had imagined.

But you had other ideas.

You picked up a lump of dough.

You rolled it into a ball.

And then you did what no recipe had ever suggested.

You gave it eyes.

Two little dough-eyes, right in the middle.

Then a mouth.

A very big, very happy dough-mouth.

And then—because you were on a roll—

you gave it a great big dough-nose.

"Look!" you said.

And the grown-up looked.

There, on the table, was not a sausage.

There was a face.

A happy, silly, lopsided face made of food.

Nana laughed.

"Who's that?" she asked.

And then she did something wonderful.

She made one too.

Two dough-eyes. A wobbly mouth. A big round nose.

Now there were two faces on the table, smiling at each other.

The table was turning into a whole silly family.

"How about some eyebrows?" Nana said.

"What are eyebrows?" you asked.

"This!" she said, and she rolled two little caterpillars and placed them above her face's eyes.

It looked so silly.

You laughed so hard the dough-nose of your face wobbled.

"Now," Nana said, "what should our faces have for hair?"

Spaghetti, obviously.

Long, curly, wiggly spaghetti, draped over each little face like a wild mane.

"Grrr!" you said, shaking yours.

The faces had become monsters.

Happy monsters. Silly monsters. Food monsters.

And the game stretched on.

They got different mouths—a surprised O, a sleepy one.

They got different noses—a round one, a pointy one, a honkin' great one.

You named them.

You made them talk to each other in silly voices.

The whole kitchen table had turned into a little stage.

And the food? The food had turned into faces that made you giggle until your tummy hurt.

The moment passed, the way all good games do.

The faces went back into balls.

The dough went back in the tub.

But for a while there—

the quiet afternoon had been full of laughing at nothing at all.

Just some dough, some "eyebrows," and a whole lot of "grrr!"

That's the thing about silly faces.

They don't need a reason.

They don't need to be food, or art, or anything.

They just need to be funny.

And funny was exactly what the afternoon needed.

## 😄 Mini Moment Reflection

After your silly-table game, sit together and wonder:

What was the silliest face you made?

What happens when dough gets a big nose?

Who was the funniest face — yours or Nana's?

What other everyday things could we turn into faces?

And then:

What makes your happiest silly face come out?

## 🍝 Let's Make a Food Family

Grab some soft dough (or playdough) and see what your hands make.

Here's your invitation — no recipe needed:

👀 Roll two eyes for every face.
👃 Add a nose — round, pointy, or enormous.
😄 Give each face a different mouth.
🌭 Try hair from rolled strips, or curly bits, or straight bits.

Then bring them to life:

Name each face.
Make them talk to each other.
Give one a giant "grrr!" and one a sleepy "z-z-z."

Notice what makes you laugh.

The best faces are almost always the lopsided, wobbly, unexpected ones.

## 📸 The Face Family Portrait

Your silly food faces deserve a picture.

Try this:

📸 Put all your faces in a row like a family photo.
😄 Add your own face into the lineup — the silliest one wins.
🖼️ Take a photo, and later you can draw a portrait of your favourite face.

Give the face family a name:

"the Grrrs"
"the Noses"
"the Wobbly Ones"

Then you'll always remember the afternoon the table grew a family.

## 🌱 As You Grow...

When you're little, a face is just a ball with eyes, and it's the funniest thing in the world.

As you grow, you might begin to wonder: How do I make the dough look real?

Then: How do I show a feeling just with a mouth?

Then: Why did that face make everyone laugh?

Maybe one day you'll learn about how eyes and mouths and eyebrows tell us how someone feels.

Maybe you'll discover that the little spaces between the features do the most talking.

Maybe you'll notice that a silly face has always been a quick way to make a room lighter.

The dough stays dough. But the laughter grows with you.

And that's what makes this moment worth remembering.

Because someday, you might look at a photograph of a row of lopsided dough faces, and the memory of laughing until your tummy hurt will come flooding back.

And maybe someone will tell you: "You could find the funny in anything when you were little."

Maybe that silly face was the beginning of a gift for making people smile.

Maybe it wasn't.

We don't have to know. We just have to laugh.

## 😄 The Tiny Moment

It wasn't part of anyone's plan.

No one had scheduled a comedy show.

It was simply... a lump of dough that got given two eyes.

But someone stopped.

Someone made a face back.

Someone let the table turn into a stage.

And for a little while, an ordinary afternoon filled up with laughter at nothing at all.

That's the magic of childhood.

Sometimes the best comedy in the world is made out of food and a silly nose.

Sometimes all it takes to find it is a wobbly mouth, a giant "grrr!", and someone who will laugh along.
